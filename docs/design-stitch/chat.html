What are the primary clinical considerations when an EGFR T790M resistance mutation is detected in non-small cell lung cancer post-first-line TKI therapy?
auto_awesome

The detection of an EGFR T790M mutation represents the most common mechanism of acquired resistance to first- and second-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), occurring in approximately 50-60% of cases [1].

Key Clinical Considerations:

  • Therapeutic Pivot: The presence of T790M strongly indicates a switch to a third-generation EGFR TKI, notably Osimertinib, which has demonstrated superior progression-free survival compared to platinum-pemetrexed therapy in this specific setting [2].
  • Testing Modality: Confirmation via liquid biopsy (ctDNA) is often preferred initially due to lower invasiveness, but a tissue biopsy is recommended if plasma testing is negative, to rule out false negatives [3].
  • Co-occurring Alterations: Consideration must be given to potential emerging co-mutations or phenotypic transformations (e.g., small cell transformation), which may necessitate alternative strategies.
